1 Verdict

Is it worth pursuing? Yes — as a second line, started small.

Your instinct is right. Almost every Greek business runs on Excel, Viber and someone's memory. Nobody is offering to fix that in plain Greek, done for them, with a price that is obviously worth it.

You are your own proof: CRM, chats, reminders, morning brief, backups, auto‑posting — all of it runs by itself now. That story sells better than any brochure.

Three conditions

  • It must not eat the website business or the ads launch. Max 1 day a week until 3 clients pay.
  • One pilot first — the homeware company you know. Get a real number (hours saved), then sell.
  • Sell hours and money, never "AI". The client buys «η Μαρία δεν κάνει πια 6 ώρες Excel», not technology.

What «unlimited» means here (careful!)

With websites a change takes 10 minutes. Here a «small change» can be a whole new system. So:

  • Unlimited fixes and adjustments to automations already running — included.
  • New automations = a new mini‑project inside the plan (e.g. one new per month in the middle package).
  • Write every automation's scope in one paragraph the client signs. That paragraph protects you.

7 How they use it

How will clients actually use it?

Three layers — from «they touch nothing» to «they ask for things». Most clients mostly live in layers 1 and 2.

1

Runs by itself

Scheduled jobs read their systems and send a message, a report or an alert — exactly like your morning brief and watchdog. They just read it on their phone. Nothing new to learn.

2

A screen they use

One simple page per company, in their colours, works on the phone — like your CRM. Stock alerts, orders to chase, one‑click actions. It reads (and only when agreed, writes) to their ERP. See the demo.

3

Asking for changes

A request chat — like your client Chats. They write «θέλω η αναφορά να βγαίνει και ανά πωλητή», you and Claude do it, usually the same day. Included in the monthly fee.

«Will they have Claude like I do, to change things themselves?»

Short answer: they can have Claude to ask questions — but you keep the building and changing. Here's why, and the three options:

Recommended start

A · You are their Claude

They ask in the request chat, you and Claude do it. Simple, safe, and it's what they pay for. Nothing breaks because nobody untrained touches it.

Easy add‑on

B · Claude for questions

The owner/managers get a Claude business seat connected read‑only to their data (reports, Drive, a copy of the numbers), with a short «manual» you write. They ask anything; they can't break anything.

Big clients, later

C · A trained power user

One person on their team gets Claude + a written playbook + training from you, and makes small changes. You review before anything touches the ERP.

8 How we build it

How it works underneath — in plain words

The pieces (the same ones we use for Advon)

  • The way in: their ERP's web‑service/API, their eshop's API, their Google or Microsoft account, or — if nothing else — a shared folder where the Excels already land.
  • The worker: small programs that run on a schedule (every morning, every hour) on a server in the EU. Like the Netlify scheduled functions we use for reminders and the watchdog.
  • The screen: one page per company, like the CRM — fast, on the phone, their logo.
  • The messages: e‑mail, Telegram, or Viber copy‑paste texts (the same pattern as your reminders).
  • The brain: Claude, for reading messy e‑mails/PDFs, writing summaries in Greek and answering questions.
  • The safety net: backups, logs of everything it did, and a watchdog that tells you if something stopped.

Safety rules for every client (non‑negotiable)

  • Start read‑only. A separate ERP user with only «read» rights.
  • Nothing writes into the ERP without a written OK, and every write is logged.
  • Data stays in their systems; copies only where needed, in the EU.
  • Signed contract + DPA (GDPR processor agreement) before touching anything.
  • They own their code and accounts. You leave a written manual — «what happens if Angelo disappears».
  • Never change the ERP itself — only talk to it. The ERP's support company stays responsible for the ERP.
  • Check the AI tool's business terms before promising «your data isn't used for training» (Anthropic's business plans say they don't train on it by default — confirm the current wording).
9 How to explain & sell

Don't explain it. Show it.

Never open with «AI» or «automation». Open with your phone and their pain. The whole first meeting is ~20 minutes:

Min 0–3Show your phone: morning brief, CRM, chats, reminders.
Min 3–5Ask the one question.
Min 5–12List their 5 tasks: who, how often, how many hours.
Min 12–14Pick the most painful one together.
Min 14–17Say the offer + the hours goal.
Min 17–20Next step: a sample file + who gives read access.

The script (Greek)

«Πριν σας πω οτιδήποτε, να σας δείξω κάτι από τη δική μου επιχείρηση.» (show the phone)

«Αυτά τρέχουν μόνα τους κάθε πρωί. Πριν έξι μήνες τα έκανα όλα στο χέρι.»

«Ποια δουλειά κάνετε κάθε εβδομάδα στο χέρι, που θα θέλατε να μην ξανακάνετε ποτέ;»

… let them talk, write down every task, who does it and the hours …

«Αν η Μαρία δεν έκανε αυτό, πόσες ώρες θα της έμεναν την εβδομάδα;»

«Σας προτείνω κάτι απλό: διαλέγουμε αυτή τη μία δουλειά, την αυτοματοποιώ σε δύο εβδομάδες χωρίς χρέωση, και αν δεν γλιτώνει τις ώρες που είπαμε, δεν πληρώνετε τίποτα.»

«Είναι σαν να προσλαμβάνετε έναν υπάλληλο που δεν φεύγει ποτέ, δεν κάνει λάθος αντιγραφής και κοστίζει λιγότερο από μισό μισθό.»

«Για να ξεκινήσουμε χρειάζομαι ένα δείγμα από το Excel και να μιλήσω με αυτόν που σας υποστηρίζει το ERP.»

10 questions to find the gold

  1. Ποιο ERP / πρόγραμμα έχετε και ποιος το υποστηρίζει;
  2. Τι Excel φτιάχνετε κάθε μέρα ή κάθε εβδομάδα; Ποιος;

  5. Τι ρωτάτε συχνά και περιμένετε μέρες για απάντηση;
  6. Πού χάνονται πράγματα; (e‑mail, Viber, χαρτιά)
  7. Πόσες φορές τον μήνα τελειώνει ένα προϊόν χωρίς να το ξέρετε;
  8. Πόσα τιμολόγια είναι απλήρωτα πάνω από 60 μέρες;
  9. Τι κάνει ο λογιστής σας που θα μπορούσε να έρχεται έτοιμο;
  10. Αν είχατε 10 ώρες παραπάνω την εβδομάδα, πού θα τις βάζατε;

What they'll ask — and your answer

«Τα δεδομένα μένουν στο δικό σας σύστημα. Ξεκινάμε μόνο με δικαίωμα ανάγνωσης — δεν μπορούμε να αλλάξουμε τίποτα. Υπογράφουμε σύμβαση επεξεργασίας δεδομένων (GDPR) πριν αγγίξουμε οτιδήποτε, και βλέπετε τι κάνει το σύστημα κάθε μέρα.»

10 The pilot

The 2‑week pilot, day by day

Day 0 · Sign & accessOne‑page agreement + DPA. A read‑only ERP user. Name the one person you'll work with.
Days 1–2 · Watch & measureSit with the person who does the task. Record it (screen or notes). Write the «before»: hours, steps, mistakes. Get sample files.
Days 3–7 · BuildBuild it with Claude. Test on old data first.
Days 8–9 · Run in parallelOld way and new way side by side. Compare the numbers line by line.
Day 10 · Go liveThe task now runs by itself. The person only checks.
Days 11–14 · Fix & measure the «after»Fix anything odd, measure the hours again.
Day 14 · Results meetingShow before/after numbers, screenshots, what it would save in a year. Offer the package. Ask for a one‑line quote you can use.

Pick the pilot task by these rules

  • Happens every day or every week (not once a year)
  • Someone obviously hates doing it
  • The data is already somewhere digital (ERP, Excel, e‑mail)
  • Reading only — nothing written into the ERP
  • The owner sees the result himself (e.g. his morning report)

Good first pilot for the homeware company

The morning report + stock alerts per store, built from the ERP: the owner sees it on his phone every day, so the value is visible from day 10. Then the Excel merges they do by hand.

13 Tips

Small things that make a big difference

Make the employee the hero

The person who does the Excel is your best ally — or your enemy. Present it as «σου παίρνουμε τη βαρετή δουλειά».

Measure before you build

No «before» number = no proof = no sale. Write it down on day 1, with their name next to it.

Name it after them

«Ο βοηθός της Ολυμπία» feels like theirs. People keep paying for things that feel like theirs.

Deliver where they already are

Viber‑ready text, e‑mail, phone. Not another app to download.

Reuse, reuse, reuse

Every automation you build should become a recipe. Client 5 should take a third of the time of client 1.

Monthly «hours saved» report

Every month, one line: «Αυτόν τον μήνα γλιτώσατε 46 ώρες». That line is why they never cancel.

Check for subsidies

Greece regularly runs «digital transformation» programmes (ΕΣΠΑ/Ταμείο Ανάκαμψης) for SMEs. If one is open, it can pay part of the fee — ask an accountant what's open now.

Use your website clients carefully

Most are single doctors — too small. But clinic groups and dental chains among them are warm doors.

Don't build the website first

No «Automation» page on advonmedia.com until the first case study exists. Proof first, marketing second.
